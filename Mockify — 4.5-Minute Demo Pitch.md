## Speaker script

### 0:00–0:42 · Motivation · Slide 1 · 94 words

"Walk me through that."

*\[Pause for two beats.\]*

The interviewer points at one line on your resume, "ninety percent test coverage," and waits. *\[Beat.\]* You did the work. But your mind goes blank.

Most practice tools coach how you sound. But nobody loses an offer for saying "um." *\[Let the laugh land.\]* They lose it when they can't explain what they built.

Here at ASU, four in ten undergrads are the first in their family to go to college. And over a hundred thousand study online, many around jobs and families.

So where do they practice at ten o'clock on a Sunday night?

### 0:42–1:16 · What Mockify is · Slide 2 · 76 words

That's why I built Mockify: a flight simulator for interviews. *\[Say "we built" if you present as a team.\]*

You upload your resume and pick the job you want. Mockify reads it like an interviewer would, then puts you in front of three AI interviewers, chosen for the gaps it found.

They ask, they listen, they follow up on what you actually said, and you watch them react. If you get stuck, a coach has your back.

And it's working today, on ASU's CreateAI platform.

### If something goes wrong

- **The mic or captions fail:** stop recording, then type the answer into the box. The panel reacts the same way.
- **You forget to mute before the Lifeline:** Sam starts reading his notes aloud. Click the mic and give the second try; that stops him.
- **You forget to unmute, or there's no audio:** keep going. Captions always run, so read the question aloud once.
- **A click does nothing:** refresh. The session picks up where it was.
- **You leave the script** (the Lifeline somewhere else, or more questions): it keeps working. Anything Ryan's script doesn't cover goes to the live model on CreateAI, a few seconds slower.
- **Anything worse:** switch to your recording and keep the same narration.
